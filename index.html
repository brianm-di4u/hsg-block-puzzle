      How to play

      Drag a tile piece from the tray onto the 9×9 bathhouse floor. Pieces never rotate. Fill a whole row, column or one of the nine 3×3 pools and it clears in a burst of steam.

      Each round deals three pieces. Place all three to get three more. The game ends when none of your pieces fits. A piece that has no room is dimmed in the tray.

      Scoring and heat

      • +1 for each tile you place.
      • Clearing 1 group scores 18, 2 at once 72, 3 at once 162 (18 × groups²), times the heat multiplier.
      • Every clear heats the water. Cool water takes two placements in a row that clear nothing to lose a step; warm water loses one with each. Heat multiplies clear points up to ×4 at 143°F, the springs' average temperature (NPS). At the top the meter reads "Boiling", after William Dunbar's 1804 name for them: "the boiling springs".
      • Empty the whole floor with a clear for a +300 clear-spring bonus.

      Modes

      • Classic: endless. Score carries you through the town's seven eras. Eras change the backdrop and its caption only; the game plays the same.
      • Daily Soak: everyone gets the same tufa stones and the same pieces today, and one best score per day counts.
      • Springs: vents sit on the floor. Clear a vent's row, column or pool to open it (+40 × the heat multiplier). Open all 47 to win. Hot Springs is usually said to have 47 springs; one source says 43 still flow, and NPS says "about four dozen".
      • Difficulty (Settings): Relaxed serves more small pieces and keeps the rescue deal strong; Master serves more big ones and thins it. It applies to Classic and Springs, each difficulty keeps its own best, and Daily Soak and Spring Trail are the same for everyone.
      • Spring Trail: hand-made puzzles with a goal and a fixed set of pieces. Undo is unlimited there, but each puzzle earns up to three stars: fewer undos, more stars.

      Controls

      MouseDrag a piece and drop it. Or click a piece, then click the board.
      TouchDrag a piece onto the board (it floats above your finger and snaps to the nearest open spot). Prefer tapping? Turn on Tap to place in Settings: tap a piece, tap the board to preview, and tap the preview to place.
      Keyboard1 2 3 or Tab pick a piece · arrows move · Enter/Space place · U undo · H hint · M mute · P pause · Esc cancel or pause

      Undo: one per round by default (change it in Settings). Stuck? The lightbulb button (or H) shows one good move, once for each tray, in Classic, Daily Soak and Springs.

      Sources

      The facts in the tickers, cards and Records come from National Park Service (NPS) pages (Maurice Historic Display Spring; History & Culture; Hot Springs & North Mountain Trails; a 1920 brochure), USGS tritium and carbon-14 dating, the Encyclopedia of Arkansas, and Wikipedia (Bathhouse Row; Hot Springs National Park; Hot Springs, Arkansas). Each card keeps its own source in the game files.

      About the pictures

      The backdrops are game art of downtown Hot Springs seen from the hills above Central Avenue and Bathhouse Row, one per era, from the wild creek valley before 1804 to today. Times of day, seasons and small details are artistic; landmarks follow sources listed in the game files. Terrain: USGS 3D Elevation Program (public domain).
