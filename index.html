      How to play

      Drag a tile piece from the tray onto the 9×9 bathhouse floor. Pieces never rotate. Fill a whole row, column or one of the nine 3×3 pools and it clears in a burst of steam.

      Each round deals three pieces. Place all three to get three more. The game ends when none of your pieces fits. A piece that has no room is dimmed in the tray.

      Scoring and heat

      • +1 for each tile you place.
      • Clearing 1 group scores 18, 2 at once 72, 3 at once 162 (18 × groups²), times the heat multiplier.
      • Every clear heats the water (back-to-back clears warm cool water faster; each opened vent adds +1 heat; dissolving a tufa stone scores +20 × multiplier). Below 102°F cool water takes two dry placements in a row to lose a step in Classic and Daily Soak (Springs cools faster on dry moves — dropping 2 steps from 102°F up — to balance +1 heat per opened vent). Heat multiplies clear points up to ×4 at 143°F, the springs' average temperature (NPS). Reaching 143°F ("Boiling", after William Dunbar's 1804 name: "the boiling springs") grants one dry-placement Boil-Hold grace before cooling begins.
      • Empty the whole floor with a clear for a +300 × heat multiplier clear-spring bonus.

      Modes

      • Classic: endless, exclusive home of the 7-era climb (0 to 4,000) and Era Postcards, transforming the backdrop, music and ambience; higher scores also bring trickier pieces up through High Flow (5,500) and Peak Flow (8,000).
      • Daily Soak: today’s board is made on this device from your clock’s date under a date-seeded Era of the Day, with 8 opening tufa stones (+150 bonus and +1 heat when all 8 dissolve), escalating rounds, Bronze/Silver/Gold par milestones, and the exact same board-independent pieces for anyone playing that date. A new board comes at your midnight, and one best score per day counts; finish without Undo or Hint for a Pure Soak badge.
      • Springs: a 3-Act Valley of Vapors geothermal surge (Acts I–III across 0–19, 20–35, and the Final 10 surge at 36–47). Clear a vent's row, column or pool to open it (+40 × heat multiplier and +1 heat). Open all 47 to win. Hot Springs is usually said to have 47 springs; one source says 43 still flow, and NPS says "about four dozen".
      • Difficulty (Settings): Relaxed serves more small pieces and keeps the rescue deal strong; Master serves more big ones and thins it. It applies to Classic and Springs, each difficulty keeps its own best, and Daily Soak and Spring Trail are the same for everyone.
      • Spring Trail: 12 Bathhouse Row landmarks plus 12 Spring Science mastery boards — pure spatial deduction with a visible Up Next piece queue, zero arcade Score or Heat, and unlimited Undo graded by stars (earn Row Walker on Ring 1 and Trail Master on all 24).

      Controls

      MouseDrag a piece and drop it. Or click a piece, then click the board.
      TouchDrag a piece onto the board (it floats above your finger and snaps to the nearest open spot). Prefer tapping? Turn on Tap to place in Settings: tap a piece, tap the board to preview, and tap the preview to place.
      Keyboard1 2 3, Q/E, or Tab pick a piece · arrows move · Enter/Space place · U or Ctrl+Z undo · H hint · M mute · P pause · Esc cancel or pause
      GamepadD-pad or left stick moves · A places · B undoes (or goes back) · LB/RB pick a piece · Y hint · Start pauses

      Undo: one per round by default in Classic and Springs (change it in Settings), fixed at one per round in Daily Soak. Stuck? The lightbulb button (or H) shows one good move, once for each tray, in Classic, Daily Soak and Springs.

      Sources

      The facts in the tickers, cards and Records come from National Park Service (NPS) pages (Maurice Historic Display Spring; History & Culture; Hot Springs & North Mountain Trails; Traditional Baths; Experience the Water; Superior Bathhouse; Ozark Bathhouse; a 1920 brochure), USGS tritium and carbon-14 dating, the Encyclopedia of Arkansas, Wikipedia (Bathhouse Row; Hot Springs National Park; Hot Springs, Arkansas), and the history pages of the Arlington Hotel and the Superior Bathhouse Brewery. Each card keeps its own source in the game files.
